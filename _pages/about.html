Hello — I’m Muhammad Owais
I am a Ph.D. researcher in Computer Science at Tokyo Metropolitan University (TMU), Japan, working on AI-driven analysis and security of social networks. My research lies at the intersection of Natural Language Processing, Machine Learning, and Social Computing, focusing on understanding and mitigating harmful and automated behavior in online platforms. I develop Transformer-based models and Large Language Model pipelines to analyze social media text, user behavior, and interaction dynamics at scale — enabling AI systems to move beyond text generation toward solving real-world societal challenges such as bot detection, coordinated manipulation, and misinformation analysis.
🚀 What I Work On
  • Transformer & LLM representations for tweet- and user-level modeling
  • Temporal behavioral analysis of evolving online accounts
  • Network-aware learning for coordinated activity detection
  • Explainable AI for trustworthy automated decision systems
Rather than viewing social media purely as text, I treat it as a complex dynamic socio-technical system where language, timing, and interaction patterns reveal behavioral intelligence.
🌍 Why This Matters
Online platforms increasingly influence public opinion, political discourse, and social trust, yet remain vulnerable to automated influence campaigns. My goal is to design next-generation AI systems capable of understanding not only what is said, but also who says it, how, and within which context, contributing toward safer and more transparent digital ecosystems across multilingual environments.
🧠 Research Philosophy
  • Data-driven — grounded in real social behavior
  • Robust — resilient to noisy and adversarial environments
  • Explainable — enabling human trust in AI decisions
  • Socially Responsible — designed with societal impact in mind
My work bridges advanced machine learning with human-centered understanding of online communication.
